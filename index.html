Core Research Thesis
"When predictions fail — why?"

Most recommendation systems optimize for accuracy. My work focuses on the 20% of outcomes that accuracy can't explain — surprise hits, hidden gems, structural biases baked into the data long before any model sees it. Fame explains only 10.4% of a track's variance. The other 90% is where the interesting questions live.

01 Data & ML Projects
Project 01 · Data Science · Music

What Drives Music Popularity?

Behavioral Discovery & Exposure Bias Analysis

Analyzed 6,000+ Spotify tracks to challenge the assumption that audio features drive popularity. Built a residual-based diagnostic model revealing that artist reputation (R²=0.104) vastly outperforms audio signal (R²=0.003), and that ~20% of tracks behave unexpectedly — indicating systematic over- and under-exposure by genre.

Project 02 · ML · Recommender Systems

MovieLens Behavioral Recommender

Popularity Bias Diagnosis & Matrix Factorization

Built a full matrix factorization system from scratch (NumPy only, no ML library) on 25M ratings across 162K users and 59K movies. Phase 1 revealed structural bias: just 11% of users drive 50% of all ratings. Phase 2 exposed the accuracy–diversity paradox — NDCG@10 of 0.95 while catalog coverage collapsed to 0.07%.

Project 03 · Behavioral Analytics · UX

Festival Drop-off Analysis

Real Interaction Log Analysis · LUCAUS 2025

Analyzed real user interaction logs from a university festival website (LUCAUS 2025). Identified that stamp-game participants showed 3.5× higher engagement than general visitors — but hit a "completion cliff" the moment the game ended. The interface treated completion as a journey endpoint rather than a reward phase, killing momentum at the highest-value moment.

03 About

Why behavior, not accuracy?

I study systems that fail users without failing metrics. A recommender can hit NDCG@10 of 0.95 while covering only 0.07% of available content — technically excellent, behaviorally broken. My triple major in Psychology, English Literature, and Technology-Art gives me an unusual vantage point: I can read both the statistical signal and the human meaning behind it.

My long-term goal is Applied Scientist at a music or media platform — building systems that understand not just what users choose, but why they hesitate, what they never get the chance to discover, and how platform design shapes the possibilities they see.

Currently at Chung-Ang University (Seoul), preparing MS program applications with music recommendation systems as my portfolio's core theme.
